Berlin has a long history as the capital of Prussia, the German Empire, the Weimar Republic, and the Third Reich. After World War II the city was divided; West Berlin became an island of Western democracy within East Germany, while East Berlin served as the capital of the German Democratic Republic. The fall of the Berlin Wall in 1989 and German reunification in 1990 transformed the city into a unified metropolis of culture, politics, and innovation.

Munich, known in German as München, was founded in 1158 by Henry the Lion. Today it is home to some of the world's largest and most renowned companies including BMW, Allianz, and MAN. The city hosts the annual Oktoberfest beer festival, which draws over six million visitors from around the globe. Munich's Marienplatz square, the English Garden — one of the largest urban parks in the world — and the BMW Museum are among its most beloved attractions.

Hamburg, officially the Free and Hanseatic City of Hamburg, has been a major trading hub since the Middle Ages and was a founding member of the Hanseatic League. The city's port, the Hamburg Docks, remains one of Europe's busiest. The Speicherstadt — the world's largest warehouse complex — is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site filled with museums, design studios, and cafés. The Elbphilharmonie, opened in 2017, has become an architectural icon and one of the finest concert halls in the world.
